# Phase 6.4: passes, buffers that last, imported images (design)

Written 2026-10-22, before building, from [the design of phase 6](engine-phase-6-design.md) (J8: "6.4 is multi-pass and persistent buffers"), EN-05, EN-06, EN-14, PH-12, PH-14 and RS-08 of [the features list](plugin-system-features.md), and
the code as it is (the engine's `plan.rs`, `frame_renderer.rs`, `effect_shaders.rs`, `scene.rs`, `worker.rs`, and `src/isf/`). Tags as there: **[V]** verified in the code or by running something; **[D]** decided by the maintainer;
**[P]** my proposal, which stands unless it is objected to; **[B]** my belief. Everything runs on software adapters only (SF-01).

## 1. What there is, and what this piece has to add [V, read 2026-10-22]

* The reader already reads `PASSES` (`TARGET`, `PERSISTENT`, `FLOAT`, `WIDTH`, `HEIGHT`) and `IMPORTED`; the rewriting step already binds **every image the shader names** (the image inputs, then the targets of the passes, then the imported
  images) at `1 + 2k` (texture) and `2 + 2k` (sampler), and the registry refuses a shader that has passes or imports and says so (`what_cannot_be_run_yet`).
* The engine does **not** yet have: a bind group layout with more than one picture (`ForeignEffectPass` has exactly bindings 0, 1, 2), more than one pass for a shader (`KindOfEffect::passes()` is 1 for a shader; the blur's second pass is
  `EffectStep::SecondPass`), a texture that lives longer than a frame (the pool gives textures back after every frame), or any idea of which frame came before (`FrameDescription` is a function of itself; the worker caches finished
  frames by its fingerprint).
* A call of a shader already carries `TIME`, `TIMEDELTA`, `FRAMEINDEX` and `PASSINDEX` (the first four numbers) and the engine adds `RENDERSIZE` after the 64 (`write_the_numbers_of_a_call`).

## 2. Pieces

| piece | what | gate |
|---|---|---|
| **6.4.1** *(built 2026-10-22)* | **passes, no memory between frames**: a shader with `PASSES`, buffers it draws into and reads by name, `WIDTH`/`HEIGHT` expressions, `FLOAT` | the spike's two-pass blur equals the same blur done by the engine's own blur (to the rounding of two passes of a half float); a real ffmpeg export, colour by colour; the preview equals the export |
| **6.4.2** *(built 2026-10-22)* | **buffers that last** (`PERSISTENT`) in the engine, and the export (sequential, exact); a seek **resets** (nothing better yet; it says so) | a feedback shader's frame *n* from a run of frames 0 to *n* is the same in the preview's engine and in the export; making the frame *n* twice gives the same picture; a seek resets; no memory is held for effects that are gone |
| **6.4.3** | **the preview of effects that depend on earlier frames (EN-14)**: checkpoints, catching up for at most 300 frames after a seek, the two modes, the three global settings | after a seek the preview shows what the export shows for that frame, if a checkpoint is near; "approximate" is said when it is not |
| **6.4.4** *(built 2026-10-22, before 6.4.3)* | **imported images** (`IMPORTED`), kept inside the shader (PH-14; K15 says why not as resources) | a shader that reads an image of its own, from a file next to it, in the export; the project reopens with it |

Each is committed, pushed for CI, and reported alone. 6.4.2 does not make the preview right after a seek, and I will not call it done for the preview until 6.4.3; the export is right from 6.4.2.

## 3. Decisions

| # | Decision | Why | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| K1 | **The engine is told a shader's structure when it is given the shader**, next to the WGSL: how many images it reads and what each is (the layer's own picture, a buffer, an imported image), the buffers (persistent? float?), and the passes (which buffer each draws into, and the two expressions of its size). `publish_effect_shader(key, source)` stays (a shader with no structure is one pass reading one picture, as phase 1 made it); a new `publish_effect_shader_with(key, source, structure)` is what the ISF host uses. The structure is plain data defined **in the engine**, which does not know ISF; `src/isf` makes one from the header. | The engine must make the passes, the textures and the sizes; the plan and the renderer already own all that. Keeps `engine` free of ISF. | [P] |
| K2 | **Sizes are expressions the engine evaluates per call.** The expression is read by the ISF host into a small tree (numbers, `$WIDTH`, `$HEIGHT`, a name of one of the shader's float, long or bool inputs, `+ - * / %`, brackets, unary minus, and the functions `floor ceil round abs min max pow sqrt sin cos exp log log2 sign`), kept in the structure, and evaluated by the engine with the call's numbers and the size of the picture the effect works on. A size is a whole number of at least 1, at most 8192 (the card's limit if that is lower); an expression that cannot be read makes the shader **unusable, with the place of the mistake**; one that gives a number that is not finite is a size of 1. | One place decides; the engine has the call's numbers. [B: ISF's own evaluator takes more than this; the corpus will say if a file in the wild needs more.] | [P] |
| K3 | **Each pass is a step of the plan** (the blur's second pass already is): `EffectStep::LaterPass { call, pass }` for the 2nd pass on, and every pass has a slot of uniforms of its own, because `PASSINDEX` (the 4th number) and `RENDERSIZE` are different in each. The engine writes `PASSINDEX` into the 4th number **only for a shader whose structure says it is an ISF shader** (a shader of phase 1 owns all its numbers). `RENDERSIZE` is the size of the target the pass draws into. | What exists already; the least new machinery. | [P] |
| K4 | **A pass with a target draws into that buffer; a pass with none draws into the picture the effect makes**, and the last pass is the one that makes it. A shader whose **last pass has a `TARGET`** is **not run yet and says why** [B: the reference host's behaviour is not something I could check; the corpus will say whether any file does it]. A **buffer written by two passes** is not run yet either. A buffer that is read before it is drawn in this frame reads **what it held when the last frame ended** (a persistent buffer) or **clear** (one that is not). | Defined behaviour for every case, a refusal where I do not know the reference's. | [P] |
| K5 | **A buffer is `Rgba8Unorm` unless `FLOAT` is set, and then `Rgba16Float`** (not 32 bits: a 32-bit float is not filterable on every card without a feature, and the engine's pictures are half floats already). It holds what the shader wrote, as it wrote it (the premultiplied question of the last piece is the same: a shader's output is taken as premultiplied). A non-float buffer **clamps and rounds to 8 bits**, as the reference does, which is what makes a feedback with a decay stick as it does there. | The look of a feedback shader depends on it. **Deviation, said:** 16 bits where 32 are asked for; where the corpus shows it matters I will use the feature. | [P] |
| K6 | **The images of a shader are bound by the order the rewriting step gave** (`image_names`), and the layout of a pass is made for that many: `ForeignEffectPass` makes the layout and the pipeline layout **for a count of images** (1 to 7 are allowed; more is not run and says so: 15 bindings is the least a card gives per stage with room to spare) and keeps them. The first image of a *filter* is `inputImage`, which is the picture the effect works on. A *generator* has no such image and starts at its buffers. | The rewriting step is already built for it. | [P] |
| K7 | **A buffer's texture is drawn into by one pass and read by the later ones**: the plan says, for each pass and each image slot, **which texture it reads** (the layer's picture, the buffer as it is now, or the buffer as it was at the end of the last frame). Non-persistent buffers come from the pool (their size is the pass's) and go back after the frame; persistent ones are kept (K8). | One rule, in the plan, testable without a card. | [P] |
| K8 | **A persistent buffer is two textures, `latest` and `older`**, kept by the engine under the **identity of the effect** (K9). The pass that draws into it draws into the one that is not `latest` and then they swap; reads of the buffer in the frame, before the pass that draws it, read `latest` as it was. **A state remembers the frame it was made at**, and what a call does with it depends on its frame *f* and the state's *n*: *f = n + 1*: go on from `latest`; *f = n*: the same frame again (a repaint, or a frame asked for twice): go on from `older`, so that it makes the same picture and does not advance; *f = 0*, or any other *f*: **start clear** (the first frame of a clip, a loop, or a seek: 6.4.3 makes the last better). A buffer whose size changes starts clear. | Two textures are enough to make a frame twice the same, and bound the memory by the number of effects, not by the frames. | [P] |
| K9 | **The identity and the frame come with the call**: `EffectCall` gets `state: Option<EffectState { id: u64, frame: u64 }>`, set by the scene for a shader that depends on earlier frames and by nobody else (the fingerprint of other frames does not change). `id` is made from the **effect instance and what it is on** (the instance ids are the project's own); `frame` is the effect's clock's frame (`EffectClock::frames_at`: since the clip began, or since the timeline began for a track or a group), so that **a clip that is cut in two starts its second part clear, because its effect is a new instance**. | The engine stays a function of what it is given. | [P] |
| K10 | **A frame that has an effect that depends on earlier frames is never taken from the worker's cache and never put in it.** The fingerprint of a frame cannot say what the frames before it were, and an edit of an earlier frame would show the old picture. | Correct first; the cost is a render on every repaint of such a frame [B: small beside what the shader costs]. | [P] |
| K11 | **The memory of states is bounded**: a total of at most 256 MiB (not the global setting of EN-14, which is for checkpoints), the least recently used state goes first, and the whole store goes when the device is replaced. A state not asked for in 600 frames made goes too. | A clip that is deleted must not keep its buffers. | [P] |
| K12 | **The export is sequential and exact by construction** (it makes every frame in order from the first), and nothing of EN-14 touches it. A project whose export skips frames (it does not now [B, to be checked in `video_export`]) would be refused for such a shader. | EN-14. | [D] |
| K13 | **The properties box says "depends on earlier frames"** for such an effect, in the words of EN-06, beside the line that says what a shader is (no new control). | EN-06. | [D] |
| K14 | **EN-14's seek handling is 6.4.3** and is designed there, with what 6.4.2 teaches; its three settings go in the global settings (`settings_file`), none in the project (UI-11). | One thing at a time. | [D] |
| K15 | **Imported images (6.4.4) are kept inside the shader's source, not as resources** *(revised when it was built, 2026-10-22: the first design, resources of the project with their own embed/link/ask question, would have needed a new kind of resource, an effect value naming it, and its own question for every shader)*: when a shader is added from a file, the images its header names are read from beside the file (the folder of the shader or below it; never an absolute path or `..`), each a PNG or a JPEG of at most 4 MiB and 4096 pixels across (at most 6), and become a part of the kind of effect (`ShaderSource::images`, in the project file's snapshot as base 64: format 17), which the key of the kind is made from too. The engine is given them as **pictures it is sent** (like those of layers, by an id made from the kind and the place of the image), named by the call (`EffectCall::images`), made so that **the shader reads the codes of the file** (the curve of no curve, the primaries of the working space: no conversion). A shader that is not given an image its header lists is **not run and says which**. | The least new machinery, nothing to ask, and the project carries what it uses as it does the text. **The cost:** the same image in two shaders is stored twice, and a project file is a third bigger for base 64. RS-08 (nothing found by a path on someone else's computer) is kept: the project has the bytes. | [P] |

## 4. What I do not know and will say when I find out

* **The reference's handling of a last pass with a `TARGET`, of two passes with one `TARGET`, and of reading a persistent buffer in the pass that draws it** [B]: K4 and K8 are what I think the files in the wild mean. The golden corpus (J9) is where a
  wrong guess shows, and only you can run the reference.
* **How much of ISF's expression language a real file uses** [B]: K2 is the usual subset.
* **Speed**: a feedback shader costs a render per repaint (K10), and a multi-pass one costs its passes. Measured when it exists, on the software adapter only.

## 5. Refinements found in the code before building 6.4.2 (2026-10-22, read, not run)

* **K9 was not enough: the identity of a state must have the clip in it.** `EffectInstanceId` is the place of an effect **in one chain** (`FIRST` is the first effect of every clip, track and group), not a number of the project; and the effects of a tag or of a track are
  done to *each* clip that they reach, with one instance. So the id of a state is made from **the owner of the effect, its instance and the clip of the layer it is run on** (`Layer::clip`; for a clip group, a track group or an adjustment clip, the span's owner).
* **K12 was wrong as it stood: the export is only exact if no layer that has such an effect is left out of a frame.** The plan leaves out the layers behind the front-most video that covers the picture (`composition`), and a layer that is left out for some frames
  would not go on and would start clear when it is seen again. **A layer that has an effect that depends on earlier frames is never left out for being hidden** (it is drawn, and what covers it covers it). That costs the draw of a layer that cannot be seen; it is what
  makes the export what it says.
* **K8's three cases are one rule with `base` and `target`**: *go on* (frame = n + 1): read `latest`, draw into `older`, and swap; *the same frame* (frame = n) and *start clear* (anything else): read `older` (cleared for a clear start), draw into `latest`, no swap. Both leave `older` as the state before the frame, so
  that the same frame can be made again. Bookkeeping is done **after** the frame was recorded without a mistake; a mistake drops the states it touched.
* **A pass that reads a persistent buffer before the pass that draws it reads `base`** (the state before the frame), and a pass after it reads what was drawn: `ImageSource` gets a third kind for it, and the plan, not the renderer, says which (K7).
* **The preview drops frames** (only the newest `Render` of a batch is made): that is a seek as far as a state can tell, which is what K8 does with it (a clear start) until 6.4.3 catches up.

## 6. 6.4.3 (EN-14) is not built, and why (2026-10-22, read, not run)

6.4.2 makes the export exact and the preview **start the effect again after a jump** (what EN-14 calls *Warm-up only*, with no marker and no setting). The rest of EN-14 is **Bounded**: checkpoints of the state, and after a seek a **simulation forward from the nearest checkpoint for at most 300 frames**. I read the code for it and stopped, because **the simulation needs the pictures of the frames it passes through, and the preview has no way to get them**:

* The preview's pictures come from `PreviewPictures`: one ffmpeg stream **per track** that keeps up with the playhead and gives *the current picture* (and thumbnails while it is behind); it cannot be asked for frame *n − 200*. `preview_content` is built from that, for the one frame at the playhead.
* The export has what is needed (`video_export::frames::FrameDescriber::describe(timeline_frame)`, with its runs of decoded video), but it is built for a whole sequential export, from an `ExportPlan` and the ffmpeg toolchain, and is not something the preview holds.
* The engine cannot do it alone: its state at frame *n* is a function of the *pictures* and values of the frames before, which it was never given.

**What can be built without that, and is not worth much on its own:** the three global settings (UI-11), checkpoints (a copy of the state every *interval* frames, within a byte budget, dropped when the project is edited, since an edit makes them wrong), and **restoring the nearest earlier checkpoint on a jump and marking the picture *approximate*** (EN-14 itself says what to show when no checkpoint is near enough). That is the fallback of *Bounded*, not *Bounded*.

**What *Bounded* itself needs, three ways (my opinion first):**

1. **A background catch-up with the export's describer** *(recommended [B, moderate])*: on a jump, a worker thread builds a `FrameDescriber` for the project as it is, makes the frames from the checkpoint to just before the playhead (decoding the videos for them with their own ffmpegs, as an export does), and sends them to the preview's engine as frames that are made and not shown; the preview shows the approximate picture meanwhile and the true one when the catch-up is done; an edit or another jump cancels it. About 1500 lines and the most careful threading of the program; it reuses what the export does and is exact.
2. **Catch up only effects that need no video** (generators, and filters of a picture that is still): their frames are made from the plan alone, with no decoding, which is every generator with feedback (the commonest use). Smaller (about 600 lines) and leaves a filter over a moving video approximate after a jump.
3. **Leave it at warm-up, with the marker and the settings** (the three settings, the checkpoints and the approximate marker, and no catch-up): the least, and *Bounded* would be a name for something it is not.

I need your word on which. Nothing else of phase 6.4 waits on it.
