## 1. 6.8: the plugin manager [P unless said]

### What there is [V, read 2026-10-22]

* *Edit > Sound plugins...* opens a window (`sound_plugins_window.rs`) with two tabs: *Plugins* (folders to look in, folders with libraries that plugins need, *Scan again*, what the scan found, the libraries that failed) and *Bundles*. The scan
  is a thread (`sound_plugins_flow.rs`) that runs the scan **program** on every `.so`/`.dll`/`.clap` of the folders and gives `LibraryReport`s; the plugins that can be effects become the installed effects (`set_installed_plugins`), and the menu of
  the + button offers them under the heading of their kind. Plugin libraries are **code**, so a folder is asked about once (`plugin_trust`); a folder that the program fetched into is trusted.
* A shader is **data**: `Add effect from file...` takes one, reads it, compiles it, and the project keeps its text (format 16). Nothing finds shaders by itself, and the menu of the + button lists a shader only after one was added (J4 of phase 6:
  "no folder is scanned and nothing ships in the box until you say so"). **The maintainer has now said so.**

### Decisions

| | | why |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | **The window is called *Plugin manager*, the menu item *Plugin manager...*.** The code's names follow (`PluginManager*`, the files `plugin_manager_*.rs`); the scan of sound plugins keeps its names (`sound_plugins_flow.rs`: it is about sound plugins). | [D] "rename ... to just plugin manager". A window that is about shaders too must not be called after sound in the code. |
| R2 | **One list of folders for everything**: the folders a person added, the usual places, the folder of the plugins that came with the program and the folders that the *Bundles* tab fetches into are looked in **for sound plugin libraries and for shaders** (`.fs` and `.isf` files, found below a folder to a depth of 4, at most 3000 files looked at in all). The usual places for shaders are those that ISF's own README names: macOS `/Library/Graphics/ISF` and `~/Library/Graphics/ISF`, Windows `%ProgramData%\ISF`; there is none on Linux (nothing is made up). | [D] "include ISFs in it ... alongside LADSPA and CLAP". One list is what a person expects of a manager. |
| R3 | **No question is asked about a shader's folder.** A shader is data (J3 of phase 6): the worst it can do is waste the card's time, which the engine's guard stops (EN-09). | UI-12: no prompt that need not be. |
| R4 | **The scan of shaders is in the process, on the thread that scans**, after the scan program has done the libraries (the shaders are read and compiled in the worker that already compiles them, with its time limit); a scan again does both. The folders list and *Scan again* serve both. | UI-09: what failed must be seen with the reason. |

| R9 | **Reading a shader file is one function in `isf`** (it was in `shader_flow`, behind a window's command): `isf::kind_of_a_shader_file(path)` reads the text, the images beside it (K15 of 6.4: the folder of the shader or below it, at most six, PNG or JPEG), makes the kind and says what is wrong, in words. The window's *Add effect from file...*, the generator's chooser and the scan all use it. | One place for the rules about what a shader file may be. |

## 2. 6.9: the ISF bundles [P unless said]

### What was found [V, 2026-10-22: the files were downloaded and read, each in a folder of its own, never run]

* **The one collection with a licence that allows it is Vidvox's own, [`Vidvox/ISF-Files`](https://github.com/Vidvox/ISF-Files)**: MIT (`LICENSE`, "Copyright (c) 2018", the name left blank), at the newest commit `395072d` of 2026-06-04: **327 `.fs` shaders** (and 38 `.vs`, which are
  vertex shaders of particular programs, and 7 pictures), a header of JSON each, all of which `isf::read` reads. **68 of them are transitions that came from gl-transitions** ("Automatically converted from ..."), each keeping its own `// License: MIT` line and
  author, so there is no need to convert gl-transitions (the repository's own `LICENSE` says nothing; its files say MIT one by one, and Vidvox has already done that conversion).
* **Not every file in it is MIT.** What a file says of itself, read by a script (`classify`, kept in the notes of the research) and then by eye:
  `Boxinator.fs` says *Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial-ShareAlike 3.0* (**not allowed**); `Inverted Page Curl.fs` and `Stereo Viewer.fs` carry BSD notices (3-clause and 2-clause, **allowed**, and kept in the file); 59 + 7 files say `License: MIT`
  (gl-transitions); 5 more have the MIT licence of Ashima Arts' noise or of Felix Turner's *Bad TV* in them. Six are **ported from Shadertoy or from the code of Inigo Quilez** (`ASCII Art`, `Smoke Screen`, `Perlin Transition`, `Broken LCD`, `Dirty Lens`,
  `Smudged Lens`), whose default licence is the non-commercial one; **44 name another person's code** ("adapted from", "ported", "based on <an address>", a blog, Stack Overflow) and say no licence of it; `Brick Pattern` and `Video Bricks` come from the Book of
  Shaders; the shaders credited to `mojovideotech` are Creative Commons non-commercial; four bring pictures of their own whose licence is not said.
* **The rule that follows** (LG-02, widened by the maintainer on 2026-10-20: the permissive licences, and GPL and LGPL that are compatible with GPLv3): **a shader is in the bundle only if what the file says of its licence is an allowed one, or the file
  is credited to the people of the repository (Vidvox, zoidberg, vade, v002, Carter Rosenberg, or no credit at all) or to an individual who gave it to the repository, names no other source of code and no licence that does not allow it, and brings no pictures** (a gift to
  a repository with an MIT `LICENSE` is under it: GitHub's terms say so, and I believe it [B, 85 %]). Anything else is **left out, and the reason is in `docs/bundleable-isf-shaders.md`**. This leaves **280** of the 327 that may be bundled.
* **The program runs 157 of those 280 today** (the corpus test, which reads, compiles, installs and *draws* each on the software adapter): 132 filters and 25 generators; **63 are transitions** that wait for 6.6, 20 draw their last pass into a buffer (not run yet), 8 read more than 7
  pictures, 8 read a second picture that is not the layer's, 10 do not compile (audio inputs, `_imgRect` variables, a construct of GLSL that naga's front end does not take), 2 have a pass whose size names an input. **Each of them says why**, and none is in the manifest
  until it runs. *(After 6.6, 218 of the 280 run, 61 of them transitions: section 3.)* (The corpus also found a real mistake in the host: a point was multiplied by the picture's height over 1080, and 79 of the 90 points of these shaders are fractions of the picture. Fixed, with a regression test: commit `da9d097`.)

### Decisions

| | | why |
|---|---|---|
| B1 | **A second manifest, `bundled-plugins/shaders.json`, kept in the program** like the first, with its own checks (`plugin_bundles::shaders`): per set an id, a name, the commit it is of, the authors, the licence as an SPDX expression of the licences of its files, the home page, **the licence texts** (each with an address and a checksum), where the source is, and **every file**: address, sha256, size, the name it is given, its licence and the name of the shader for a person. The checks: every address is https; every file has a checksum and a size that is at most `MOST_BYTES_OF_A_SHADER`; every name is a plain `.fs` file name; every licence is an allowed one (`licence_is_allowed`); no name twice. | The same policy as for plugins, written once. |
| B2 | **The addresses are of single files at a commit** (`raw.githubusercontent.com/Vidvox/ISF-Files/<commit>/ISF/<name>.fs`), **not of an archive**: the bytes of a file at a commit do not change, and an archive that GitHub makes is not promised to be the same twice. | Found when thinking about the checksum; an archive's would one day not match. |
| B3 | **One curl, many files**: the files of a set are fetched by one run of `curl` given a config file (`url` and `output` for each), so that the connection is kept where the server lets it (measured on this computer: 374 files, about 120 ms a file, 45 s in all; and the Windows build of curl failed 6 of 6 when run with `--parallel` and its `schannel` revocation checks, which a plain run did not); each is checked against its checksum **before** it is used, and a set is built in a folder of its own and put in place only when all of it is there (as for plugins). `--ssl-revoke-best-effort` is given **only on a second try, after a failure that says "revocation"** (curls before 7.70 do not know the flag), because the check that a certificate was not revoked cannot be made on some networks, and **the checksum, not the connection, is what says the file is the right one**. | Measured 2026-10-22; the Windows revocation failure is a fact of this machine. |
| B4 | **A new folder, `fastforwardrs/shaders` in the data folder of the person** (next to `plugins`), is where the *Bundles* tab puts shaders (a folder for each set, with the `NOTICE.txt` and the licence texts); it is one of the folders the scan looks in. It is not the folder of plugins, which is trusted as code. | R2, R3. |
| B5 | **The *Bundles* tab has a section for each kind**: *Sound plugins* (as it was) and *Shaders*, each with its own button, question, progress, cancel and message. A person who wants only the shaders (about 0.8 MB) is not made to download 145 MB of plugins. The question names the number of shaders, their size, the sites they come from and the folder, and says that each file is checked against a checksum kept in the program. Nothing is downloaded until the question is answered yes. | The permission given on 2026-10-22 is to download shaders; what a person downloads is still their question. |
| B6 | **`docs/bundleable-isf-shaders.md`** is the record: the repository, the commit, the licence of each file and the reason for each left out. `NOTICE.txt` in a set's folder says who wrote the shaders, that the files are unaltered, and where the source is. `docs/distribution-notices.md` and the README say what a copy of the program must carry. | LG-02, DS-notices. |
| B7 | **The bundle holds only shaders that run**: the manifest is made by a script (`tools/bundled_plugins/make_the_shader_manifest.py`) from the corpus's own list of what it drew; an ignored test (run by hand when the manifest changes, as the one for the plugins is) fetches every file from the network, checks each against its checksum and has the corpus read, compile and draw each. | A shader in the manifest that does not run is a bug that reaches a person. |

## 3. 6.6: transitions [P unless said]

### What there is [V, read 2026-10-22]

* The reader tells a **transition** (`startImage`, `endImage`, `progress`) from a filter and a generator (`Shape::Transition`); the registry **refuses to run one** (`what_cannot_be_run_yet`), and `structure_of` gives a picture other than `inputImage` no slot. 63 of the shaders that the corpus found to be good and that may be bundled
  are transitions (68 in the collection: they are what Vidvox converted from gl-transitions), and wait for this.
* The engine's plan puts **all the layers of a container in one pass** (`PlaceLayers`, which clears the picture first and draws them back to front), after **all their effects**, which run in passes of their own on each layer's picture; so **nothing can read what is behind a layer**:
  the picture that the layers are put together in does not exist until the one pass that makes it.
* A shader layer is **never taken for one that covers what is behind it** (`changes_each_pixel_by_itself` is false for every shader), so what is behind a layer that has a transition is not left out of the frame for being hidden [V, `composition/pictures.rs`].

### Decisions

| | | why |
|---|---|---|
| T1 | **A transition is an effect of the clip on top.** `endImage` is **the clip's picture** (after the effects before the transition in its chain), `startImage` is **the picture of everything behind the clip in its container** (the *backdrop*), and `progress` is an ordinary value with a stopwatch, which the person animates from 0 to 1 over the overlap. What the shader makes is the clip's picture for the rest of its chain, and the clip is then put in its place **over** what is behind it, as any clip is. Nothing is made up: the effect is put on with `progress` at its own default (0, so that the clip is not seen) until it is animated. | [D] "putting one clip on a track above another and applying an effect to the clip on top." The rest is how that can be made to mean something. |
| T1a | **A transition is done in the space of the frame, not of the clip's own picture** [found 2026-10-22, reading `describe.rs` to see where the clip's effects are made; **this corrects the first draft of T1, which said "the clip's own picture"**]. A shader reads its two pictures by the coordinates of the picture it draws, so a clip that is not the whole frame (a logo in a corner, a video that is turned or smaller) would be given the *whole backdrop squeezed into the logo's own rectangle*, and at `p = 0` there would be a small copy of the frame where the logo is, and not what is behind it. So **the describer puts the clip (with the effects before the transition) into a group of one layer, which the engine makes a picture of the size of the frame of (it is how a generator is made already), and the transition and the effects after it are the effects of that group's layer, put over the whole frame**. `endImage` is then the clip as it is in the frame (clear where it is not), `startImage` is the frame behind it, and both are the same size and place. For a clip that is the whole frame nothing is different to look at, and it costs one pass more and no more pixels than the clip's own picture (the transition is made at the size of the frame, not of the clip's picture: a 4K clip in a 1080 preview is a cheaper pass). | T2's arithmetic is only true when the two pictures lie on each other. A group of one layer is what the engine and the describer already have for "a picture the size of the frame that something is put in"; nothing new is needed of the engine for it. |
| T2 | **Why "over" is right, by arithmetic [V, then by a test]**: with an opaque backdrop `A` (the frame's own always is: it begins as opaque black) and the clip's picture `E` (premultiplied, opacity `a`), the shader's `mix` makes `O = (1-p)A + pE` with opacity `1-p+pa`; the clip is then put over `A`: `O + A(1-opacity(O)) = A + p(E - aA) = (1-p)A + p(E over A)`. **At `p = 0` the picture is exactly what is behind, at `p = 1` exactly the clip over what is behind, and between them the two mixed**, also where the clip is partly clear or smaller than the picture. | So the transition needs no "replace" blending, and a clip with a transition at `p = 1` is the clip, not a cut out of one. |
| T3 | **Where the backdrop is not opaque it is not exact**, and **no transition is run there**: in a *clip group* or a *track group* (whose picture begins clear: the groups are drawn into pictures of their own) the effect is **counted as one that is not run** (`effects_not_run`), and the box of the effect says so. A transition on a **track** is not offered (a track has no backdrop that is a thing), nor on an adjustment clip (it has no picture of its own); a **tag**'s effects are done to each member clip, so a tag may have one. | Said, not guessed at. A person who puts the clip in a group gets a note and not a wrong picture. |
| T4 | **The plan** [simplified from the first draft, which split the chain of the layer at the transition: the effects before the transition do not read what is behind, so they can as well be run late as early, and the layer keeps **one pass of effects**, so that everything that is kept by the layer (the pictures of its steps, the buffers of its states) stays as it is]: the effects of a layer that has **an effect that reads the backdrop and runs** are not run with the effects of the others, before anything is put in its place, but **when the layers behind it have been put in their places** (clearing; or, if there are none, when the picture has been cleared to opaque black): then its whole chain runs, and **the effect that reads the backdrop reads what is there** (converted into the codes of a screen by a step of its own, as a picture is: `EffectStep::Backdrop`, right before the call, which a pass of its own makes into a picture and which does not change what the next effect works on); and the layers **from that one up to the next layer that reads the backdrop** are then put in their places over what is there (`PlaceLayersOver`, which does not clear). Where no layer reads the backdrop the plan is what it was, pass for pass. | The least change to what plays today: nothing that has no transition is planned differently, and a test says so. |
| T5 | **The shader**: a new `ImageSlot::Backdrop` and `ImageSource::Backdrop`: `startImage` is the backdrop, `endImage` is the picture (as `inputImage` is for a filter); `EffectsThatRun` says which keys read the backdrop. A shader that reads `startImage` and not `endImage` (or the other way) is not a transition and is not run (it says so). **The engine does not know "transition"; it knows a shader that reads the backdrop**, and runs one only on a layer of the frame itself (T3). On a layer that is not the whole frame the backdrop is read by the coordinates of the layer's own picture (what ISF says of every picture it is given); the describer (T1a) is what makes the layers that have a transition the whole frame. | The two images are told apart by their names, which is all ISF says. |
| T6 | **Offered**: a transition is in the + menu of a clip (video, picture, text, generator), under *ISF* and its category, and in the list of the plugin manager as a *transition*. `EffectKind::applies_to_a_clip_of_kind` / `applies_to_a_track` / `applies_to_a_group_of_kind` say T3. | |
| T7 | **The corpus**: the 63 transitions are drawn (over a picture as their backdrop and another as the clip's) and the manifest is made again. | PH-15. |
| T8 | **How the describer knows**: `PictureOperation::Shader` has a `reads_the_backdrop` (made by the composition from `EffectKind::is_a_transition`, as `state` is made from `depends_on_earlier_frames`), so that `describe.rs` need not ask the registry; the describer splits the calls of a clip at the first of them that reads the backdrop (among the calls that are made, so that a transition whose images are not ready does not leave the effects after it in the wrong space), and wraps as T1a says. The same for a generator (its group is the inner layer). **A tag's or a group's transition** is the same effect on each clip, or on the group's own layer (a group is a picture of the size of the frame already, so it needs no wrapper). | One place that decides, and a test of each of the shapes of layer. |

### What was built, and what was found [V, 2026-10-22]

* **The engine** (`plan.rs`, `shader_steps.rs`, `shader_structure.rs`, `frame_renderer.rs`): `ImageSlot::Backdrop` and `ImageSource::Backdrop`; `EffectsThatRun::add_shader_that_reads_the_backdrop`; `EffectStep::Backdrop` (a step of its own that
  makes the backdrop into codes, right before the call, which does not change what the next effect works on); `PassPlan::PlaceLayersOver` (a pass that does not clear); the effects of a layer that reads the backdrop run when the layers behind it
  have been put in their places, in the frame's own container only (in a group the effect is counted in `effects_not_run`). A frame in which no layer reads the backdrop is planned pass for pass as it was (a test says so).
  **47 tests of the plan, which need no card** (`transition_plan_tests`, `plan`, `shader_steps`), **and 13 of the card** (`transition_tests`, on the software adapter, in the compatibility mode and in linear light) whose oracles do not depend on the colours: *at progress 0 the
  frame is the frame of the layers behind, and at progress 1 it is the layer over them* (T2), for a clip that is see-through too; in between, the mix of the codes, and the arithmetic of "over" in the compatibility mode.
* **The ISF host**: `startImage` is the backdrop and `endImage` the layer's picture; a shader that reads one and not the other, or `inputImage` as well, is refused with the reason (`registry.rs`); the rules of where a transition goes are in `EffectKind` (clips that are seen, tags and groups: yes;
  a track and an adjustment clip: no).
* **The describer** (T1a, T8): a clip, or a generator, that has a transition is made a group of one layer over the whole frame, the transition and what comes after it being the group's effects (`frame_scene/describe.rs`); `PictureOperation::Shader` has `reads_the_backdrop`.
  **From the project to the pixels** (`video_export::frames::tests`): two picture files on two tracks, the transition on the top one, `progress` keyframed from 0 to 1 over forty frames, drawn by the engine in linear light: red, the half-and-half mix, green. And a clip group that has the transition (the
  picture of the group over what is behind it), and a tag (each clip of it is made a group).
* **The window**: the box of a transition says what it reads, how it is used and where it is not run; *Add effect from file...* with a transition on a track says why not.
* **What the corpus found, and was fixed** (a bug of the host, and not of transitions: it was only that these shaders are full of it): **naga drops the initialiser of a global that is not a constant, without a word**, so `float ratio = RENDERSIZE.x / RENDERSIZE.y;` made `ratio` zero, and every
  transition that begins so drew nothing right (Circle Crop was black at every progress; Pixelize did not even compile). The rewriting step now declares such a global without its initialiser and gives it by a generated function that runs before the shader's `main` (`rewrite.rs`; a `const`, an array and what is
  inside a condition of the preprocessor are left as they are). Regression tests at the level of the text and of the pixels. **Measured:** of the 280 files of the corpus, no shader that drew before fails now, and Pixelize runs.
* **The corpus now draws transitions** (over a picture of its own, at progress 0, 0.5 and 1) **and says whether each begins as what is behind it and ends as the clip** (by the mean of its colours): of 62 transitions, 61 do, and are in the manifest (218 shaders now: 132 filters, 25 generators and 61 transitions); the one that does not
  (Crazy Parametric Fun, whose `smoothness` has no `DEFAULT`, so it divides by nought) is left out with the reason, in `docs/bundleable-isf-shaders.md`.
* **44 planted mistakes, all noticed** (plan, steps, structures, the host, the rules, the card, the describer, the box, the globals).

## 3.1 What is not done [P]

* **No automatic keyframes**: a person animates `progress`. (A button that does it, from the start of the clip to the end of the overlap, is a convenience that can come later; the maintainer did not ask for it.)
* **No transition between two clips of one track** (end to start): the maintainer's way is one clip over another.
* **No transition inside a group that has effects** (T3): the effect is not run there, and says so. (A transition into a group's picture would need the group to be drawn over what is behind it first: not asked for.)

## 4. 6.4.3b: the lead-up on a seek (EN-14) [P unless said]

### What the maintainer chose [D]

The checkbox *Generate lead-up on seek* of the View menu (a global setting, on to begin with): **on = option 1** of section 6 of `engine-phase-6-4-design.md` (a background catch-up with the export's describer), **off = option 3** (checkpoints of the
state and a marker that says the picture is approximate, with no catch-up). The checkpoint interval and the memory they may take are global settings too (built in 6.4.3a).

### What there is [V, read 2026-10-22]

* A state (the buffers of a shader that last from one frame to the next) is kept by the engine under an id, with the frame of the effect's clock that it was made at, and **a frame that is not the one after the last starts it clear** (`effect_states.rs`: `GoOn`, `SameFrame`, `StartClear`).
  The preview is therefore exact only while it plays on from a frame that it made, and after a jump it starts every such effect again.
* The engine makes **only the newest frame of a batch of requests** (the preview's `Render`); the export's requests (`RenderForExport`) are all made, in order. A state is a function of the pictures and values of all the frames before it, which only the **export's describer**
  (`FrameDescriber`, frames in order, one decoder for each run) can supply for a frame that the clock is not at.
* The preview's describer makes a frame from `PreviewContent`, one frame at the playhead, from `PreviewPictures` (one stream for each track, current picture only).

### E1: option 3, the checkpoints (6.4.3b-1)

| | | why |
|---|---|---|
| E1.1 | **A checkpoint is a copy of the state of an effect at a frame**, taken by the engine every *interval* frames of the effect's own clock (a frame whose number is a multiple of the interval, when the frame has been made without a mistake), into a store that is **bounded by the memory budget** (the oldest go first). It is a copy on the card (a texture copied to a texture); nothing comes back to the processor. | EN-14: "checkpoints of the effect's state ... every 60 frames ... 1 GiB, the oldest dropped." |
| E1.2 | **On a jump** (a state asked for at a frame that is not the one after its last) the engine **restores the nearest checkpoint at or before the frame that is asked for**, and goes on from there as if the state had been made by the frame before this one: it is *approximate* (a state that is not the exact one for the frame: the buffers are those of an earlier frame). With no checkpoint the state starts clear, as it does now, and is approximate too. | EN-14: "shows the nearest state, marked ..., until playback has caught up". |
| E1.3 | **A state is approximate from the jump until 300 frames of its own have gone on** (the length that the lead-up is at most, fixed: EN-14); a frame says whether any state it used is. The frame event of the engine carries `approximate`, and the preview shows **a line on the picture while it is true** (never otherwise: it is not a control that is always there): *"An effect depends on earlier frames: this picture is approximate until playback has caught up."* | EN-14 asks for the marker. |
| E1.4 | **Every edit, a new project, a load, a change of the working space or of the size the preview is made at drops every checkpoint** (the window says so to the engine: `forget_the_checkpoints`), because a checkpoint is made from what was there, and a checkpoint of what is not there any more is a wrong picture. The size of a checkpoint is the size of the state's buffers: one that is not the size of the buffers that are asked for is not used. | The open design issue of 6.4.3 noted in the plan. |
| E1.5 | **The export is never touched**: it makes every frame in order from the first and takes no checkpoint and restores none (it has no jump). | EN-14. |

### E2: option 1, the lead-up (6.4.3b-2)

| | | why |
|---|---|---|
| E2.1 | **On a jump, when the checkbox is on and the frame has an effect that depends on earlier frames, a worker thread makes the lead-up**: it builds a `FrameDescriber` for **the project as it is** (a copy, at the size the preview is made at and in the colour it is made in), and describes **every frame from the nearest valid checkpoint (or the clip's start, or 300 frames before the jump: whichever is nearest) up to the one before the playhead**, sending the pictures and the frames to the engine **as frames that keep their state and are not shown** (a new request, `RenderForState`, that is never dropped and makes no pixels). | The maintainer's choice; the export's describer is what knows the frames before. |
| E2.2 | **Meanwhile the picture is made without the states** (the effects that depend on earlier frames are drawn with an empty state that nothing keeps), marked approximate; **when the lead-up reaches the playhead the states are used again from the frame after it** and the marker goes. If the clock went on while the lead-up ran (playing), the lead-up goes on to the frame the playhead is at (it decodes far faster than the clock goes), and only then hands over. | The states that the preview and the lead-up keep must not be mixed: two makers of one state would start each other clear. |
| E2.3 | **A lead-up is cancelled by an edit and by another jump** (a flag the worker looks at between frames, and a number on each request so that the engine drops what is left of a cancelled one). What it made is **never trusted after an edit**: the states that it made are dropped with the checkpoints (E1.4). | A state of a project that is not the one on the screen. |
| E2.4 | **Checkpoints are taken by the lead-up too**, so that a second jump to near the same place is a short lead-up. | |
| E2.5 | **The test that says it is right**: a project with a feedback shader and a real video: the preview's frame *n* after a jump, with the lead-up done, **is the export's frame *n***, pixel for pixel, on the software adapter; and a jump to a frame inside 300 frames of the clip's start has the same state as playing from the start. | The whole point of EN-14. |

### Order and size [B]

6.4.3b-1 (E1) is about 800 lines with tests and is built first: it is what the checkbox off does, and what E2 uses. 6.4.3b-2 (E2) is the larger (about 1200) and has the threads: a worker, a flag, the engine's new request. Each is committed and reported alone.
