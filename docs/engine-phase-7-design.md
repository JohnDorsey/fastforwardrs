# Phase 7: the sound engine and LADSPA (design)

Written 2026-10-18, after phase 4, **before the code** (the standing rule for the engine phases). It is the design of phase 7 of [the plan of action](plugin-system-plan-of-action.md): AU-01, AU-03 to AU-09, AU-12, DS-04, DS-05, SF-04 to SF-10, UI-12, PL-04, and the
parts of ID, MD, RS and MG that sound effects need, of [the features list](plugin-system-features.md). CLAP (AU-02) is **not** designed here; section 7 says why and what it needs.

Tags: **[V]** verified in the repository or by running something; **[D]** decided by the maintainer; **[P]** my proposal, which stands unless it is objected to; **[B]** my belief or estimate.
Nothing here has been run yet: this is a design, and no code of the phase exists.

## 0. What phase 7 is, in one paragraph

Today the sound of the preview and of the export is made by **one ffmpeg filter graph** (the same text, run twice): every clip is cut out of its file, sped up, given its volume and made exactly as long as its place on the timeline, the clips of a track are joined, and the tracks are mixed. There is no way to put
anything in that chain that ffmpeg does not have. Phase 7 replaces the graph's **joining, volume and mixing** with a **block graph in the program** that the preview and the export both call, keeps ffmpeg for what it is good at (decoding files, `atempo`), adds **sound effects** as a kind of effect (the descriptor, the chain, the keyframes and the file already
exist: they are shared with the picture effects), and makes the first of them **LADSPA plugins**. It is also **where native code first loads into the program**, so the whole safety design of section 14 of the features list (the prompts, the approvals, the scan in a child process, declining as a safe state) is built here **before any binary is loaded**.
It is the phase with the most risk: it changes everything that is heard, and nobody has judged the sound by ear (README, *Status*: "audible has not been judged by ear").

## 1. What is there today [V, read in the code, 2026-10-18]

* **The sound plan** (`composition/sound.rs`): `plan_sound(project, from, is_usable)` gives a `SoundPlan` of **strips**, one per track that has sound, front first. A strip is `SoundItem`s one after another with no gaps: `Silence { frame_count }` or `Clip(SoundClip)`. A `SoundClip` has a source, the source frames heard, the speed (clip times track), how many timeline frames it is heard for, and a
  `Loudness`: `Constant(factor)` or `Changing(points in decibels at frames, evenly between)`; the clip's volume and its track's are already together. A clip whose mute switch (or its track's) is turned by keyframes is cut into the stretches heard and not heard. **Nothing in the plan says anything about effects.**
* **The graph** (`media/export/filter_graph/sound.rs`, 379 lines): every source is made into **f32, 48000 Hz, stereo** and split into a copy for each clip that uses it; a clip is `atrim`-med at `frame/fps` seconds, treated by the sped-up setting (`atempo` chains up to 100x each, `asetrate`+`aresample` for "increase pitch" up to 1000x, silence beyond or when the sped-up sound would be under 0.1 s),
  multiplied by `volume` (a constant, or `pow(10,(dB(t))/20)` with `eval=frame`, **per ffmpeg frame and not per sample**), mixed onto silence of the clip's exact length (`amix` + `volume=2`: because `apad` gave frames without timestamps for a clip past the end of its file), the items of a strip `concat`-ed, every strip padded to the whole length, and the strips mixed
  (`amix=inputs=N` then `volume=N`, which is a **plain sum**; nothing limits it). The graph is written to a file and given to ffmpeg with `-filter_complex_script`.
* **The preview** (`playback/audio_supplier.rs`, `media/audio_stream.rs`): an `AudioStream` is an ffmpeg child that runs that very graph from the playhead on and writes raw f32 samples; the supplier queues them for the device a little ahead (0.3 s), skips late sound, holds early sound back with silence, restarts when the playhead jumps, and when a *change to what the sound is made of* happens it starts a **replacement** stream that takes over a little way ahead of the speakers
  with a fade. The device (`audio_device.rs`, cpal) takes from the queue on its own thread and gives silence when the queue is dry. A separate module (`playback/sound_effects`) makes the program's own little sounds (the snip, the drawer) and does not go through any of this.
* **The export** (`video_export/encoder.rs`): the encoding ffmpeg is given the picture as raw video on stdin and the **source files of the sound as its further inputs**, with the same graph; it writes video and sound into one file.
* **Sound effects exist only as a word.** `EffectDomain::Sound` is in the descriptor; `EffectKind::applies_to_a_clip_of_kind` says a sound effect can be on **an audio clip** (not a video, even though a video has a soundtrack) and `applies_to_a_track` is true for everything; a clip group or a track group cannot have one, a tag can. A project file can keep one that the program does not have (a missing effect) and write it back [V]. Nothing runs one.
* **`#![forbid(unsafe_code)]` is at the top of the crate** (`src/lib.rs`), and nothing in the tree uses `unsafe`. **Loading a plugin needs it** (a dynamic library, and calls through C function pointers). This is the first decision (G1).
* **No native code is loaded by the program today.** ffmpeg and ffprobe are child processes.
* **The test set the maintainer named (AU-12)** is the LADSPA folder of an LMMS 1.2.2 install, 119 DLLs that I checked by reading bytes (64-bit, each contains `ladspa_descriptor`). I could not find that install on this machine in the usual places [V], so I do not know its path (question 7). The CI cannot have it.

## 2. Decisions

| # | Decision | Why | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| G1 | **All the `unsafe` code lives in a crate of its own**, `crates/ladspa-host`, a member of a Cargo workspace whose root is the existing package. The editor crate **keeps `#![forbid(unsafe_code)]`**. The host crate has a small **safe** interface (open a library by path; list its descriptors; make an instance for a sample rate; connect buffers and control values; run; drop) and every `unsafe` block has a `SAFETY:` comment; the crate is a few hundred lines. The workspace change touches the CI (`--workspace`), `deny.toml`, REUSE and the README. The other way is to relax `forbid` to `deny` and allow one module: less to change, but the guarantee "this 100 000-line program has no unsafe code" would go, and a `deny` can be allowed anywhere by a later hand. | Containment; the one place to audit. | [P], question 1 |
| G2 | **The ABI facts that are easy to get wrong are in the host crate and have tests**: `LADSPA_Descriptor` is a C struct whose `UniqueID` and `PortCount` are `unsigned long`, which is **32 bits on Windows (LLP64) and 64 on Linux**, so the fields are `c_ulong` and not `u64`; `LADSPA_Data` is `f32`; port descriptors are bit flags (input/output, control/audio); range hints have bounded-below/above, toggled, sample-rate, logarithmic, integer and the three default hints (minimum, low, middle, high, maximum, 0, 1, 100, 440); `run_adding` may be absent. **A test plugin written in Rust (a `cdylib` in the repository, CC0) implements the ABI** (a gain, a one-pole filter with a control port, a stereo swap, a plugin with a toggle and an integer port, one that crashes in its constructor on request, one that reports 0 ports) so that the tests do not depend on anybody's install. | Tests that run in the CI and on a clean machine. | [P] |
| G3 | **A plugin that is scanned is scanned in a child process** (DS-04): `fastforwardrs-plugin-scan LIBRARY` loads the library, reads every descriptor, prints one JSON line (labels, names, makers, ports with their hints, the unique ids) and exits; the editor reads the line. A library that crashes, hangs (killed after 5 s as a tree, by pid, as the mutation runner does), prints nothing or prints something that is not the format is **"failed" with the reason**, and **is not tried again until its size, time or hash changes** (kept in a scan cache file in the settings folder). The scan runs in the background and never blocks the window. | DS-04; a crashing library never takes the editor down. | [D] AU/DS-04 |
| G4 | **Plugins run in the editor's process**, not in a child of their own (AU-03). A plugin that crashes while it *runs* takes the editor down: SF-04 says a plugin is trusted native code. Two things make that less bad: **(a)** a **breadcrumb file** is written (and flushed) before the first time a library is *used* in a session and removed when the program ends well; if the program starts and finds it, it says which library was in use when it ended badly and marks it failed (G3's cache) so it is not loaded again until it changes. **(b)** The autosave that exists is unchanged. Running each plugin in a child process with shared memory would remove the risk and cost latency and a lot of code; it is a later option and nothing here prevents it (the graph calls plugins through one trait). | AU-03, SF-04; honest about the risk. | [P], question 8 |
| G5 | **The block graph** (`src/sound_engine/`, no unsafe, no ffmpeg in it) works in **blocks of 512 frames at 48000 Hz, stereo, f32** (the kind the ffmpeg graph already makes everything into). Its nodes: **a clip feed** (samples that arrive), **silence**, **gain** (a `Loudness`, evaluated **per sample** from the plan's own arithmetic `linear_factor_at`, not per ffmpeg frame), **effect chain** (plugins), **strip** (items joined), **track effects**, **mix** (a plain sum, as now, with a limit only at the end, in the device and in the encoder, as now). The preview and the export call the very same graph with different drivers (a thread that fills the speakers' queue ahead of the clock; a loop that runs as fast as it can). The graph is rebuilt from the plan when the sound is changed, as the stream is replaced now. | AU-03. | [P] |
| G6 | **ffmpeg stays as the decoder, and for `atempo`** (AU-11, Q-29): a **clip feed** is an ffmpeg child that cuts the clip's part of its file with the same `atrim` and the same speed treatment that the graph has now (the code that writes those filters is kept, and its tests), and writes f32 stereo 48000 Hz on a pipe, exactly the clip's length of the file's sound (the graph pads the rest with silence: the length is the graph's job, so the old "silence of exactly this length" trick of `amix` is not needed). **One child per clip, opened ahead** (about 2 s ahead in the preview, and a few at a time in the export) and closed when it ends; a seek or a change opens the children again for the clips that are heard from the new place. [B] the cost of a process per clip is acceptable (ffmpeg starts in ~100 ms here; to be measured in 7.2 on a project with 200 clips). The time stretch can be replaced later by a native one without touching the graph. | AU-11; the smallest change that keeps every sped-up sound as it is. | [P] |
| G7 | **The null test is the gate of the graph** (AU-09): a corpus of projects (the ones that `tests/` already makes, random projects with several tracks, cuts, gaps, keyframed gain and mutes, and sped-up clips in each of the three settings) is made into sound **by the old filter graph (the oracle) and by the block graph**; they must be the same to a stated tolerance. **Expected differences, which the tolerance has to allow and which I will measure and write down, not assume:** (1) ffmpeg's `volume=...:eval=frame` changes the gain once per chunk of ffmpeg's, the block graph per sample (a keyframed gain differs by at most the slope times a chunk); (2) the position of a clip in samples (ffmpeg rounds each item's seconds; the block graph puts item *i* at `round(48000 · frames_before / fps)`, which does not drift over many items: **it can differ by a sample from the old graph, and the old graph is the one that drifts**); (3) float rounding of the sum. A constant-gain project with no sped-up clips should be **bit-identical or within 1e-6**; the rest within a bound that I will set from the measurements [B: about −70 dB]. | AU-09. | [P] |
| G8 | **The export's sound goes through a temporary file**: the sound is made first by the block graph, as f32 stereo 48000 Hz into a raw file in the temporary folder (about 23 MB a minute), and the encoding ffmpeg is given `-f f32le -ar 48000 -ac 2 -i that-file` as its second input instead of the source files and the graph. The file is removed when the export ends (or fails). The other way, a second pipe, is awkward for a child process on Windows, and a sound of an hour is 1.4 GB, which the temporary folder has to hold [B]. | Simplest robust way. | [P], question 5 |
| G9 | **Sound effects are the same objects as picture effects**: descriptors, instances, chains, badges, racks, snapshots in the file (MD-03). What is new is where they are **applied**: a clip's sound effects are applied to its sound (after the cut and the speed, **before the clip's volume**, as a DAW's insert is before the fader), a track's to the track's strip after its clips are joined, and a tag's to each member. **Video clips can have sound effects too** (they have a soundtrack; today `applies_to_a_clip_of_kind` says only audio clips can): the rule is changed in one function. A clip group or a track group cannot have one (its sound would have to be mixed first: later, with the master bus, AU-10). The sound plan gets the effect calls the way the picture plan does (`operations_at`): a list of calls with the values at each frame. | MD-03, EF-05. | [P], questions 2 and 3 |
| G10 | **A LADSPA plugin is an effect type with the key `ladspa:<library file name without its ending>:<label>`** (`ladspa:amp_1181:amp_1181`, `ladspa:tap_reverb:tap_reverb`), version 1.0 (LADSPA has none). The **unique id** is read and kept in the snapshot but is not the key (plugins in the wild share ids). The descriptor is made from the ports: a **control input** is a value (number with the range, the default from the hint, the scale logarithmic if the hint says so, **a toggle** if toggled, **a whole number** if integer, range multiplied by the sample rate where the hint says so, a range of nothing replaced by −1e6..1e6 and shown as a field to drag); a **control output** is not a value (nothing can set it); **audio input and output ports decide the channel layout**: 1 in / 1 out is made once for each channel (AU-07, two instances for stereo, each with the same values), 2 in / 2 out is made once, 1 in / 2 out and 2 in / 1 out are made once and the missing side is the mix or a copy, **0 audio inputs (a generator)** is not an effect and is listed as "not used yet" (AU-10). A snapshot whose ports are not those of the plugin found on this machine is **not the same plugin**: the effect is a missing effect (kept whole), by the rule that exists. The category is the **maker**; the library is in the hover text. | ID-01 to ID-05, AU-07. | [P] |
| G11 | **Parameters change sample-accurately** (AU-04): the graph asks for the values of an effect at the start of each block, **splits the block** at the frame of a keyframe that changes a value and, where a value moves between two keyframes, at every 32 samples; toggles and integers change at the split; a **gain-like** value (the one a plugin calls "gain", or any value in decibels whose hint is a range through 0) is **smoothed** over the block by the graph itself where the plugin does not (LADSPA has no smoothing standard). [B] 32 is fine; it is a constant. | AU-04. | [P] |
| G12 | **A seek resets**: when the playhead jumps (or a replacement stream starts), the plugins are `deactivate`d and `activate`d and run on **0.5 s of the clip's sound before the new place** (as much as the clip has; silence where it has none) with the output thrown away, so that filters and tails are in a sane state (AU-05). | G13 | **A missing sound plugin passes the sound through unchanged** and keeps its settings (AU-08, MD-07): the same as a missing picture effect, a badge, a note, a row in the export's question. **Declining is safe** (SF-07): a plugin that the person declined is a missing effect. | AU-08, SF-07. | [D] |
| G14 | **The trust model (SF-04 to SF-10), built before any binary is loaded** (piece 7.3) and tested with a fake loader that loads nothing: **(a) a linked plugin** (named by a path in the project) gets a warning prompt the first time it is needed, **remembered by path and hash of the file** in a store in the settings folder (a changed file asks again); **(b) an embedded plugin** (inside the `.ffrs`, as a resource of kind `plugin`) gets the severe prompt **every time the project is opened** (not remembered, not after an autosave recovery), with *Don't load* (the default) and *Load it*, and *Load it* is only accepted by **holding it down for 5 seconds with the mouse or holding Enter (never Space) while it visibly fills; letting go resets it**; **(c)** an embedded plugin may have **one build per platform**; the prompt names the build for this machine, and a project with no build for it shows the plugin unavailable and says which platforms it has; **(d)** it is **extracted to a cache folder after the prompt, checked against its recorded hash, loaded, and removed when the program ends**, at most **256 MiB**; **(e)** the command-line export **refuses** a project that needs an embedded plugin or an unapproved linked one, listing them, unless given `--allow-plugins`; **(f)** nothing about plugins is an always-visible control (UI-12): the prompts are modal windows shown when a project that needs them is opened or a plugin is added. Duplicate keys: the person's folder wins over the others. The scan is in the background at start and when a folder is added. | DS-01 to DS-03, PL-04. | [P] |

## 4. The tests

* **Reference first.** Every piece of arithmetic (the item positions, the gain, the sum, the split of blocks, the default of a port from its hint) is written once in Rust on `f64` and the graph is held to it.
* **The oracle stays.** The old graph is kept and the null test is a test in the suite, not a one-time check; it runs on the corpus every time. It is also what `FASTFORWARDRS_SOUND=ffmpeg` selects.
* **The test plugin is the plugin of the CI**; the LMMS set is a test that is **ignored by default** and runs on the maintainer's machine with a path given in an environment variable.
* **The safety model is tested before it can load anything**: the loader is a trait, and the tests of 7.3 give it a fake that records what it was asked and panics if it is asked without an approval.
* **Round trips of what the program does not have**: a plugin that is not installed, one whose ports changed, a library that is gone, a build for another platform, each saved, loaded and saved, byte for byte.
* **The mutation campaign** over every new module, aimed at the right test programs where the protection is in an integration test.
* **Real speakers are not in the CI**; the recording stand-ins are (as now).

## 5. What this does not change, and what is not exact

* **The pictures** are not touched. The waveforms on the timeline are made by ffmpeg as before. The program's own little sounds (snip, drawer) are not part of the graph.
* **Time stretching** stays ffmpeg's `atempo` (AU-11). A sped-up clip sounds as it does.
* **The sound is not judged by ear by me.** I can show that the new graph and the old are the same to a tolerance, and that the plugins do the arithmetic; I cannot say whether it sounds good. [B] it will, because the old graph is the reference.
* **A plugin that crashes while it runs ends the program** (G4). The breadcrumb tells the person which one next time.
* **A plugin with a long tail** (a reverb) after a seek is not what it would have been (G12).
* **No latency compensation for LADSPA** (G12).
* **No master bus, no generators** (AU-10): later; the graph has room for one more node at its end.
* **Groups** (a clip group or a track group) cannot have sound effects (phase 5's work is picture; the sound of a group needs the master bus's idea).
* **The Windows build of LMMS's plugins** may need DLLs of their own (the C runtime): untested [B]; a plugin that needs one and cannot find it is "failed" with the system's message.
* **CLAP** is not here.

## 6. Risks, in the order I worry about them

1. **The null test may not close** (G7): the old graph has quirks (frame-wise gain, rounding of seconds) that I have named and probably not all. *Mitigation:* 7.1 is only the graph and the test, with nothing switched; if it does not close, nothing was changed.
2. **A process per clip** (G6) may be slow to start on Windows in a project with very many short clips, giving gaps in the preview. *Mitigation:* the lookahead, a pool of at most 8, and the measured 200-clip project; the fallback is one child per strip.
3. **The safety model is a lot of window** (G14) and the severe prompt's hold has to be right on a pointer and a key. *Mitigation:* it is built and tested with a fake loader before there is anything to load.
4. **`unsafe` and the C ABI** (G2): a wrong struct layout is a crash, not an error. *Mitigation:* a Rust test plugin that uses the same header definitions, a layout test (`size_of`/`offset_of`) against numbers worked out by hand for both data models, and the scan in a child.
5. **Plugins from 2005 are not always safe to call** (they may write past a buffer if the port is not connected, or not accept 0 samples). *Mitigation:* every port is connected before `activate`, blocks are never empty, and the scan child instantiates and runs one block of silence as part of the scan, so a plugin that crashes does so there.

## 7. CLAP (AU-02), not designed here

The plan says CLAP "follows as a second step inside this phase". It needs its own design: the entry point and factory, `clap_plugin` with `process` and **events** (parameter changes as events with sample offsets, which is a better fit for G11 than LADSPA is), latency (AU-06, built then), state save and load (a plugin's own state, which has to be kept in the project: a new kind of value), and a GUI that the program would not host. The block graph and the trust model are the same. I would write that design after you have heard 7.6, so that it is made from what LADSPA taught, and so that the host crate and the null test are not changed in two ways at once.

## 8. For the maintainer

Decisions that I took, and the ones I would like an answer to before the code, most important first (the default is what I will do if you say nothing):

1. **Where `unsafe` lives (G1).** A crate of its own in a workspace, so that the editor keeps `#![forbid(unsafe_code)]`. The other choice is to turn `forbid` into `deny` and allow one module. *Default: the crate of its own.* (It changes the CI and the layout of the repository.)
